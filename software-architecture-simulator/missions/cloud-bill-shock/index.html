The Cloud Bill Shock

The invoice tripled; customers did not.

  • Design project
  • Advanced
  • About 40 minutes
  • Free, in your browser

Play this mission

The scenario

Finance is alarmed: this month’s cloud bill is three times last quarter’s, while traffic grew only 20%. The platform was sized for a launch that never materialised. Nobody owns the spend because nothing is tagged. You must cut the bill without degrading what customers experience: availability, latency and AZ resilience are not up for negotiation.

Context. Mature product, steady 400 rps with a daily evening peak of 1.8×. Strict SLO of 99.9% availability.

What is at stake. CFO has frozen hiring until the bill is under control.

Your brief

  • Target ≤ $2,500 / month at peak (simulated estimate).
  • Availability ≥ 99.9% and p95 ≤ 400 ms at the evening peak.
  • Must survive the loss of one AZ (≥ 99%).
  • At least 90% of spend must be attributable via cost tags.
  • No security regressions (nothing public).

What you will learn

  • Right-sizing and autoscaling
  • Storage lifecycle policies
  • NAT/data-transfer costs and VPC endpoints
  • Caching to shrink the database
  • Observability costs: log levels and retention
  • Cost allocation tags
  • Finding idle resources

Builds: FinOps and cost control, AWS services, Site reliability and observability, Solution architecture.

How it plays

You are given a running architecture and the evidence a real engineer would have: metrics, logs, traces, cost and recent changes. Form a hypothesis, make a change, deploy it, and read why the system reacted as it did. A verification load test then scores your design and a debrief explains the root cause and the alternatives. Nothing here touches a real cloud account.
